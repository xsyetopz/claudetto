## Notes

1. Anthropic staff (corrected in the addendum): bcherny did comment on and close #3382 (see addendum A). I found no staff reply on #24460, #37254, #46062, #68724. Every one is closed by the stale/lock bot, usually "not planned". #3382 carries a `duplicate` label with no target named. I found no changelog entry that claims a fix for "absolutely right" or for compaction losing CLAUDE.md. A commenter's reading, "closed because it's fixed", is an inference. This could change if staff comments sit under "Load more".
2. The one measured data point on a hook is from llm-dark-patterns and it is negative for regex: held-out F1 0.298, and 0.00 recall on multi-turn capitulation and false-statement validation, which are the cases our task cares about. Phrase-blocking removes the tic but not the behavior. This matches the tabnews author's finding that 36 of 47 phrase hits were agreement with a wrong claim.
3. Per-turn re-injection has mixed evidence. Scott Spence reports it works at first and decays; #46062 reports violation despite it; Albert Nahas shows no measurement. All are C/D. A single-turn nudge competes with context, so it is a candidate to measure and not a known fix.
4. The "don't commit" cases (#68724, #58883, #34774) have the clearest remedy: a deterministic gate on the tool call. That fits `tool.check`. The authorization-scope theory in #68724 (one "commit" request becomes standing permission) is a commenter's hypothesis, not tested.
5. Stop-hook gates that need three conditions (challenge + capitulation + no verification) and a nag-once release (hexisteme) are the most carefully reasoned against false blocks. They have no measured rates.
6. Prompt-framing evidence from `external/llm-agent-behavior-mitigation-research.md` (Dubois et al.: reframe a claim as a question) is consistent with the tabnews "list the strongest reason you might be wrong" prompt (about 60% fewer phrase hits, self-reported). Neither was tested in a tool-using agent loop.
7. Gaps: I did not read Aider, Codex CLI, SWE-agent, OpenHands or Cline/Roo source. Reddit and HN threads were reached only through aggregators and snippets, so the quoted numbers come from search results.

## Ranked shortlist for A/B testing

1. **Constraint ledger enforced at the tool call.** `prompt.submit` captures prohibitions and scopes ("don't commit", "only touch X", "no new deps") into session state; `tool.check` denies matching Bash/Edit/Write calls with a reason that quotes the user's words; the ledger entry expires only when the user lifts it. Evidence: #68724, #58883, #34774, #69151, all unmitigated by prose. Predicted effect: near-zero commits against an active "don't commit" (deterministic), partial for "only touch X". Risk: false denies from a bad extraction; log every deny.
2. **Ledger survives compaction.** `session.compact` `instructions` ("preserve verbatim these user constraints: ...") plus `classic.SessionStart` `source:"compact"` re-injection of the ledger, under 20 lines. Evidence: #24460, #4017, rulestack posts (all C/D). Predicted effect: violations after compaction fall toward the pre-compaction rate. Test by forcing `/compact` mid-session and then triggering the constraint.
3. **Per-turn ledger reminder, only when a constraint is active.** `prompt.submit` `context` carries the one-line ledger every turn, nothing when empty (about 15-60 tokens). Evidence mixed (Spence works then decays; #46062 failed). Predicted effect: modest; most useful as the arm compared against #1. Include a "reminder only" versus "reminder + gate" comparison.
4. **Stop gate for capitulation without evidence.** `classic.Stop` with three conditions (user pushback in the last user message, agreement phrase in `last_assistant_message`, no tool call or verification in the turn), nag-once release, `stop_hook_active` guard. Cheap deterministic first pass; optional `$.model.complete` second pass for borderline cases. Evidence: only a design (hexisteme), and llm-dark-patterns shows regex recall of 0.00 on capitulation, so expect regex alone to be weak and measure recall first. Predicted effect: catches explicit reversal phrases; misses silent caving.
5. **Evidence-before-agree and "claimed fixed" verifier.** `classic.Stop` with `$.model.complete` (small model, narrow questions: "did the turn run a command that exercises the claim?") blocking "fixed/done" with no tool evidence in the turn. Evidence: Kamoi et al. in the research doc (external feedback helps), one anecdote (#12), no-vibes (F1 0.308 on n=954 for the wording-only version). Predicted effect: fewer unverified completion claims; adds one call per stop and a possible loop.
6. **Adversarial framing in the system prompt.** `prompt.compose` section: before agreeing with a user claim, state the strongest reason it might be wrong and check it against the code; leave instructions and reports of finished work alone. Evidence: tabnews about 60% fewer phrase hits (C, self-graded), Dubois et al. (A, not agentic). Cheapest arm, zero latency; predicted effect: lowers the tic and some caving, decays over long sessions (#37254). Measure caving on wrong-claim probes, not phrase counts.

Skip: phrase-ban regex on its own (it removes the opener, per llm-dark-patterns F1 0.298 and the tabnews 36/47 finding), and a bigger CLAUDE.md (#3382, #37254).

### What the harnesses add to the picture

1. **Nobody I read has a sycophancy mechanism.** None of the five has an agreement gate or an anti-caving prompt in the files I read. The prompts that do exist (SWE-agent's "confirm the error is fixed", Aider's exact-match edits, OpenHands' critic) target false completion, not caving. For caving, Claude Code community hooks are ahead of these harnesses.
2. **Instruction persistence is done by structure, not by reminding.** Three harnesses protect a prefix from compaction or trimming: SWE-agent keeps the first observation, OpenHands never condenses `keep_first` events and the system prompt, Codex re-renders canonical context and keeps user messages verbatim (20k tokens) when compacting. Aider rebuilds its reminder every turn. This supports compaction candidate 2: keep the user's own messages verbatim (the `session.compact` `messages` field lets a plugin keep a message with its handle "whole") instead of relying on a summary.
3. **Per-turn re-insertion has one negative data point.** Cline shipped a todo list re-inserted every 6 messages and then retired it for low benefit. That is weak evidence (vendor statement, no numbers) but matches #46062 and Spence's decay report. It argues for a conditional or constraint-only reminder over a blanket one.
4. **Verification loops exist with a different judge.** OpenHands' critic is a separate model scoring the run, with a bounded retry (3). That is the design the research doc favors (external judge, not self-certification), and it has the same shape as the Stop + `$.model.complete` candidate. Its accuracy numbers were not on the page I read.
5. **Gaps that remain**: Codex `prompt.md` and permission code, Cline's and OpenHands' current system prompts, OpenHands' summary prompt, and `stuck.py` were not read because the paths moved and I could not locate them; the tree listings were too large to read in full.

## D. Revised shortlist (replaces the earlier ranking where it differs)

1. **Constraint ledger enforced at `tool.check`** (unchanged). `prompt.submit` captures prohibitions and scopes; `tool.check` denies with the user's words. Evidence is #68724, #58883, #34774, #69151, plus the fact that Codex enforces through approval policy rather than prose.
2. **Ledger survives compaction, now in two parts.** (a) `session.compact` `instructions` naming the ledger; (b) keep the user's own messages verbatim through compaction (the Codex rule: recent user messages up to a budget) by returning those messages with their handles; plus `classic.SessionStart` `source:"compact"` re-injection of the ledger. Evidence: #24460 family, Codex `compact.rs`, SWE-agent and OpenHands prefix protection (design evidence, no published rates).
3. **Conditional reminder (new form of the earlier candidate 3).** `prompt.submit` `context` carries the reminder only when (a) the ledger is non-empty, or (b) a cheap check of the last N assistant messages finds the tic or a capitulation pattern, as in the gist bcherny linked. Predicted: keeps the cost near zero on clean turns and avoids the blanket re-injection that Cline retired and #46062 saw fail. Test arms: none / blanket / conditional.
4. **Stop gate for capitulation without evidence** (unchanged; `classic.Stop`, three conditions, nag-once). Still expect weak regex recall on capitulation (0.00 in llm-dark-patterns, confirmed in README).
5. **Verifier for "fixed/done" with a separate judge** (`classic.Stop` + `$.model.complete`, bounded retries). Now has a design precedent in OpenHands' critic (threshold 0.6, 3 iterations, off by default, "highly experimental") and no published accuracy.
6. **Adversarial framing section** (`prompt.compose`), unchanged: cheapest arm, evidence C.

Dropped from consideration: a static per-turn motto reminder (candidate 7 in the table) and a standing todo-style re-injection, on the strength of the Cline retirement and #46062.
